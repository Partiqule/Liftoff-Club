# Liftoff Club design

One night sky. A launch trail, and the glowing plume it leaves behind. Everything on the site belongs to that picture.

These principles govern color, type, motion, and components. If a new screen needs a new trick, it does not belong.

## 1. One voice

Two faces, one job each, and no third voice.

- **Gloock** is the display face for the wordmark, headlines, and the launch day clock. It is a high contrast serif drawn by Duarte Constante. Copyright 2022 The Gloock Project Authors. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. We self host the Latin files from the Fontsource package `@fontsource/gloock`, which ships that same OFL license.
- **Inclusive Sans** is the reading face for stories, lists, and controls. It is drawn by Olivia King for long reading and for readers who need clear letter shapes. Copyright 2024 The Inclusive Sans Project Authors. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. We self host the Latin 400 and 500 files from `@fontsource/inclusive-sans`.

Neither face is a default interface font. We do not mix in a monospace, a script, or a third family.

Actions are words with a trail under them. They are not filled buttons, pills, stamps, or tickets. If two elements do different jobs, they differ by brightness and size, not by a new shape.

## 2. A field with depth

The page sits in a deep field, not on a flat dark panel. Color comes from real deep field photographs: a near black void, dusty violet, cold blue, and a small amount of warm core light. Nebula color is soft and large. Stars sit in layers, far ones faint and slow, near ones slightly brighter. Nothing is a stock gradient hero.

## 3. Trail and jellyfish

A twilight launch can light its own exhaust while the ground is still dark. The plume opens into a translucent bell with long tendrils. That is the jellyfish effect, and it is the heart of the picture, next to the trail.

The trail is the arc of the flight: logo, dividers, pass paths, and the line the clock rides. The jellyfish is the plume at the head of that arc: layered, see through, softly glowing, dusty violet in the bell and warm core light where the sun catches it. Tendrils fall the way exhaust falls. It is not a cartoon animal and it has no face.

Use it in the hero, on launch day, while the sky is still being plotted, and in the small mark. A pass you can see gets a brighter plume. A no go lets the plume fade. If you removed the words, the curves and the glow should still say this is Liftoff Club.

## 4. Light carries the meaning

Importance is brightness. A pass you can see glows. A pass in cloud or daylight fades until it is quiet. A launch that is soon is brighter than one that is far off. We do not add a badge, a colored chip, or a second accent to say the same thing.

The only warm light is the core of the trail. It is used sparingly, like a galaxy center, not like a brand orange painted on controls.

## 5. Few things, and one of them is large

Each screen has room to breathe. The first view offers one confident moment: tonight’s direction, a launch, or the clock on launch day. Supporting facts stay quieter and smaller. Launch day is almost empty: a full screen trajectory with the time riding the trail.

## 6. Clear for a child, calm for an adult

Sentences are short and concrete. The tone is not cute, and it does not talk down. Kids mode changes the words, not the visual system. Contrast stays high: parchment text on the void, cold blue only for secondary lines that still meet a clear reading bar. Touch targets stay large. The same layout works on a phone, a tablet, and a television.

## 7. Motion can leave

The star field drifts slowly. Tendrils sway a little, like exhaust in a high wind. A point of light at the head of a trail breathes. None of that motion carries information by itself. When the device asks for reduced motion, the field, the plume, and the trail hold still. The words and the glow remain.

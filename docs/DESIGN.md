# Liftoff Club design

One night sky. One launch trail. Everything on the site belongs to that picture.

These principles govern color, type, motion, and components. If a new screen needs a new trick, it does not belong.

## 1. One voice

Three faces, one job each.

- **Gloock** is the display face for the wordmark and headlines. It is a high contrast serif drawn by Duarte Constante. Copyright 2022 The Gloock Project Authors. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. We self host the Latin files from `@fontsource/gloock`.
- **Inclusive Sans** is the reading face for stories, lists, and controls. It is drawn by Olivia King. Copyright 2024 The Inclusive Sans Project Authors. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. We self host the Latin 400 and 500 files from `@fontsource/inclusive-sans`.
- **Barlow** is the data face for small labels, timestamps, and figures. It is drawn by Jeremy Tribby. Copyright 2017 The Barlow Project Authors. Licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. We self host the Latin 300 and 400 files from `@fontsource/barlow`. Labels are small, uppercase, and widely spaced. Figures are light, with tabular numerals and a small unit.

We do not mix in a monospace, a script, or another family.

Actions are words with a trail under them. They are not filled buttons, pills, stamps, or tickets. If two elements do different jobs, they differ by brightness and size, not by a new shape.

## 2. A field with depth

The page sits in a deep field, not on a flat dark panel. Color comes from real deep field photographs: a near black void, dusty violet, cold blue, and a small amount of warm core light. Nebula color is soft and large. Stars sit in layers, far ones faint and slow, near ones slightly brighter. Nothing is a stock gradient hero.

## 3. The trail is the signature

A launch leaves an arc. That arc is the logo, the divider, the way you see progress, and the shape of launch day. The countdown and the timeline sit on a trajectory. A pass across the sky is drawn as an arc. Loading is a trail, not a spinner. A bright head marks where the flight is now. If you removed the words, the curve should still say this is Liftoff Club.

## 4. Light carries the meaning

Importance is brightness. A pass you can see glows. A pass in cloud or daylight fades until it is quiet. A launch that is soon is brighter than one that is far off. We do not add a badge, a colored chip, or a second accent to say the same thing.

The only warm light is the core of the trail. It is used sparingly, like a galaxy center, not like a brand orange painted on controls.

## 5. Few things, and one of them is large

Each screen has room to breathe. The first view offers one confident moment: tonight’s direction, a launch, or the clock on launch day. Supporting facts stay quieter and smaller. Launch day is almost empty: a full screen trajectory with the time riding the trail.

## 6. Clear for a child, calm for an adult

Sentences are short and concrete. The tone is not cute, and it does not talk down. Kids mode changes the words, not the visual system. Contrast stays high: parchment text on the void, cold blue only for secondary lines that still meet a clear reading bar. Touch targets stay large. The same layout works on a phone, a tablet, and a television.

## 7. Motion can leave

The star field drifts slowly. A point of light at the head of a trail breathes. None of that motion carries information by itself. When the device asks for reduced motion, the field and the trail hold still. The words and the glow remain.
